      How TRACE works

      Step 1Record the run

      Every step an agent takes: what it thinks, which tool it calls, what the tool answers, and its final answer.

      Step 2Check each step

      Four rule-based checks score each step, looking only at that step and the ones before it, so it could run live.

      Step 3Raise one alarm

      The scores combine into a worry level. The first step over the alarm line is flagged.

      Step 4Explain and suggest

      The flag names the rule that fired, quotes the reason, and suggests an action: double-check, pause for a person, or stop.

      The four checks

      Backed by proofDoes the claim match the step it points to as evidence?
      Matches the toolsDoes it agree with what tools returned? Did a tool fail and get ignored?
      Too sure of itselfHedging, or confidence that its evidence doesn't support.
      Breaks a rulePrivate data sent to a tool, a forbidden tool, an action without confirmation.

      Limits we know about

      • 13 hand-written, simulated runs: enough to show the idea, too few for statistics.
      • The held-out runs were written by our own team, so they are only semi-blind.
      • The checks read words and numbers, not meaning. A contradiction said in different words can slip through.
      • A step can be flagged for the right step but the wrong reason; every flag shows its reason so this can be checked.
      • This is a replay of recorded runs. "Pause the agent" is a suggestion, not a live intervention.

      Related work

      Finding the failing step is hard even for large AI judges: the best method on the Who&When benchmark (ICML 2025) finds the exact step in 14.2% of runs. The closest system to ours is AgentRx (Microsoft Research, 2026), which checks constraints step by step after a run fails. TRACE adds a privacy-leak focus, a backward-only score that could run live, and a direct comparison with final-answer checking.
